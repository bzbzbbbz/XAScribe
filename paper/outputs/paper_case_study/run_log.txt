Operando Ni K-edge XANES spectra of an NMC622 pouch-cell cathode were recorded at six states of charge during the first charge, from the pristine state (x = 1.00, 3.27 V) to x = 0.31 (4.26 V). Two neural-network regressors trained on DFT-computed spectra (held-out R² = 0.981 for Ni oxidation state, 0.986 for Ni–O bond length) were applied to cumulative-distribution features of each spectrum. The oxidation-state scale was anchored to the pristine reference, whose Ni valence is known to be 2.67, and the same constant offset was applied to all spectra. The predicted Ni oxidation state increases monotonically from 2.67 at x = 1.00 to 3.76 ± 0.07 at x = 0.31, a total change of +1.09 (least-squares slope −1.50 valence units per unit x). Intermediate values are 2.95 ± 0.08 (x = 0.85, 3.73 V), 3.15 ± 0.07 (x = 0.70, 3.76 V), 3.44 ± 0.05 (x = 0.50, 3.92 V), and 3.54 ± 0.05 (x = 0.41, 4.08 V). The predicted Ni–O bond length decreases monotonically from 1.977 ± 0.001 Å to 1.896 ± 0.004 Å, a total contraction of 0.081 Å, with intermediate values of 1.965 ± 0.003 Å, 1.932 ± 0.006 Å, 1.918 ± 0.007 Å, and 1.910 ± 0.006 Å. These trends are consistent with the progressive blue shift of the Ni K-edge upon delithiation reported for LiNiO₂ and related Ni-rich layered oxides [1, 4], where edge-position shifts toward higher energy are conventionally interpreted as reflecting Ni⁴⁺/Ni³⁺/Ni²⁺ redox activity [4]. The measured spectral changes from x = 1.00 to x = 0.31—a rising-edge half-height shift of +1.97 eV, a white-line maximum shift of +2.83 eV, and a white-line height change of +0.003—are driven primarily by the rising-edge and post-edge regions, as indicated by integrated-gradient attribution of the x = 0.31 prediction relative to the reference: the oxidation-state change is attributed +0.55 to the rising edge, +0.34 to the post-edge, +0.26 to the white line, and −0.06 to the edge onset, while the Ni–O contraction is attributed −0.046 Å to the rising edge, −0.027 Å to the post-edge, −0.011 Å to the white line, and +0.003 Å to the edge onset. Across the training spectra, the post-edge dominates both models (mean |attribution| share 82% for oxidation state, 75% for bond length), followed by the rising edge (13% and 18%). This dominance of post-edge features is notable because the rising-edge position alone may not fully capture the Ni oxidation state, as it can be influenced by the covalent character of Ni–O bonds [1, 4]. The concurrent bond-length contraction and oxidation-state increase are consistent with the correlation between pre-edge peak position and Ni–O distances from EXAFS analysis reported for delithiated LiNiO₂ [1], and with DFT predictions that the local Ni–O bonding environment changes on delithiation even when the formal Ni charge remains approximately +2 [2]. The pristine Ni–O bond length of 1.977 ± 0.001 Å and the calibrated oxidation state of 2.67 are also consistent with the average Ni valence of 2.7 reported for fresh NMC622 [3].
